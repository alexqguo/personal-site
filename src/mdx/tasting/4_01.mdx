We are BACK!! After a nearly three year hiatus, the Tasting series has returned as the Tasting Atelier! We've got a new name, new website, new faces, and most importantly new drinks to try. More to come on the new format but for now let's dive right into more important matters. Today we will be sampling **BuzzBallz**, the cocktail in a <del>can</del> <del>bottle</del> weird small container thing that's been taking over your local bodegas and liquor stores (remember this!).

Founded in 2009 by Texas high school teacher Merrilee Kick, BuzzBallz were meant to address a gap in the canned cocktail market at the time. This style of drink was not rare, though it was rare to find one that was good. Keep in mind, at the time we were dealing with Mike's Hard, Smirnoff Ice, and Four Loko. Not much else existed yet! Kick wanted something strong, portable, and good tasting. Jury is still out on that last one. But at 15-20% ABV, you could say the phrase "Sippin' Kick's Ballz" may have multiple meanings. The practical nature of BuzzBallz is admirable too; the compact non-glass signature spherical form factor is not just functional but stands out. We've seen a few other similarly shaped drinks, but these Ballz were the first to stick. 

Like many other canned cocktails, the popularity of BuzzBallz rapidly increased in the last 15 years. Today, they market themselves as a fun, chaotic party drink which is especially popular for pregaming. As one might expect, a product of this nature isn't without controversies. It's a story we've seen before, namely that the appearance and marketing strategy of BuzzBallz tends to appeal to market segment of... a questionable purchasing legality. Schools have unsurprisingly reported BuzzBallz emerging on campus. To be fair, the Tasting Atelier has seen more egregious examples of predatory marketing (i.e. the majority of Seasons One and Two).

In 2024, BuzzBallz was acquired by Sazerac, makers of Sazerac Rye, Southern Comfort, Buffalo Trace Bourbon, Fireball, and more. They paid over a billion dollars for BuzzBallz! As you might expect this introduced much broader distribution capabilities- Ballz were quite literally flying all over the place!

But here's where things get interesting. As we have discussed previously in the Atelier, NY state liquor laws prohibit the sale of wine and spirits in grocery stores and bodegas, and conversely beer can *only* be sold in those establishments. This has led to human rights violations like Chateau Diana "wine product" which was featured back in [episode 11](https://www.alexguo.co/tasting#e11). So if you're a producer of a casual canned cocktail drink that can only be sold in liquor stores, what do you do? Well, have you ever been at a CVS, seen them selling Fireball, and wonder how they're allowed to do that? Look closely... very closely. It's not the Fireball we know, it's a malt liquor version! That's right, FMBs are BACK! Who makes Fireball again? I think you see where I am going with this.

And the same applies to BuzzBallz. Remember when I said earlier that they were taking over both bodegas *and* liquor stores? Yeah. There is the original canned cocktail version of BuzzBallz (which in NY would only be allowed to be sold in liquor stores) and the FMB version, which can be sold in grocery stores and bodegas.

This is known as a **brand extension** strategy, that only a company with Sazerac's resources and distribution capabilities (as well as the prerequisite brand recognition) could pull off, but it's allowed BuzzBallz to more broadly penetrate the NY market. So here in episode 44 we will be having a FMB version of BuzzBallz!! More specifically the **LIME 'RITA**, **STRAWBERRY 'RITA**, **SOUR APPLE**, and **PEACHBALLZ** flavors. 

I can only assume that when Sazerac acquired BuzzBallz they fully intended to implement this dual-recipe brand extension which was already in place for Fireball, Southern Comfort, and possibly more I have not seen yet. Expect a deeper dive into similar brand extensions, and their resulting controversies and lawsuits, in future installments of the Tasting Atelier.
